# Dialing In Light-Roast Espresso

Lightly roasted washed coffees from high elevations (such as Ethiopian Yirgacheffe or Kenyan SL28) retain dense cellular walls and lower porosity than traditional medium-dark blends. If you pull them at a flat 9 bars with a standard `1:2` ratio, you almost always end up with sharp, under-extracted acidity.

> [!tip] Quick Recipe for Dense Washed Coffees
> Try a **1:2.5 ratio** (`18g` dose $\rightarrow$ `45g` yield) with a 6-second low-pressure pre-infusion at `2 bar` before ramping to `6–7 bar`.

## Grinding for Uniformity

When dialing in light roasts, pushing the grind finer eventually causes micro-channeling—water finds narrow paths of least resistance, over-extracting bitter tannins along the channel while leaving surrounding pucks under-extracted.

| Parameter | Traditional Medium-Dark | Modern Light Roast |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dose** | 18.0 g | 18.0 g |
| **Yield** | 36.0 g (`1:2.0`) | 45.0 g (`1:2.5`) |
| **Water Temp** | 91 °C | 94–96 °C |
| **Peak Pressure** | 9.0 bar | 6.5 bar |

For the fluid dynamics behind puck resistance and Darcy's Law, see [[espresso-extraction#Darcy's Law & Puck Permeability|The Physics of Espresso Extraction]].
