# Clarity & Rhythm in Technical Essays

Great technical writing does not hide behind abstract nouns. It starts with a concrete problem, shows the exact mechanism, and uses typography—headings, blockquotes, callouts, and diagrams—to pace the reader's attention[^1].

![[typography-grid-specimen.png|Editorial Typography & Baseline Rhythm Specimen — click to zoom and pan]]

> "Typography exists to honor content. In a world rife with unsolicited messages, good typography is a quiet invitation to think."

## Principles of Readable Technical Prose

1. **Show the failure mode first** before presenting the abstraction that solves it.
2. **Keep line lengths between 60 and 75 characters** (`max-width: 700px` at `106.25%` base font size).
3. **Pair serif body copy (`Source Serif 4`) with crisp monospace (`JetBrains Mono`)** so code identifiers stand out effortlessly without visual clutter.

> [!question] When should a diagram replace a paragraph?
> Whenever a paragraph describes more than ==three interacting state transitions== or spatial relationships, a diagram or table lets the reader verify the invariant at a glance.

[^1]: Inspired by Robert Bringhurst's *The Elements of Typographic Style* and Leslie Lamport's notes on structured proofs.
